## Who does what

<div class="lanes not-content">
  <div class="lane tech">
    <h3>You</h3>

      <li>Start CretLink and choose <strong>Give help</strong>.</li>
      <li>Read the six digits aloud when they appear, and confirm that they match.</li>
      <li>Ask your AI agent to do the work, and watch what it does.</li>
      <li>Stop the session when the work is done.</li>

  </div>
  <div class="lane cust">

    <ol>
      <li>Starts <code>CretLink-Support.exe</code> and answers <strong>Yes</strong> when Windows asks.</li>

## On the same network

Their program finds your computer by itself. When it does not, read them the **connection code**; they type it under their list of technicians.

## Not on the same network

Before the visit you need [a relay](/CretLink/relay/) that your copy knows, and [a customer copy](/CretLink/get-started/#a-copy-for-a-customer-on-another-network) that knows the same relay.

1. Press **Use relay…**. Your copy now waits on the relay.
2. Read the **relay code** to your customer: sixteen characters in four groups.
3. Your customer types it into the same field as a connection code. Their screen names the relay it connects through.

A relay code works once, for as long as your waiting screen is open. Like a connection code it only says where to meet: you still compare the six digits aloud, and your customer still decides. Nothing connects again by itself. When the relay stops or a connection is lost, the session ends on both sides, and both screens say so.

## Put your AI agent to work

Once the customer has allowed control, the agent you connected sees CretLink's remote tools. Ask it in your own words, for example:

> Use CretLink to find out why the Wi-Fi adapter on the customer's computer reports an error. Tell me what you find before you change anything.

Three habits make a session go well:

- **Let it look first.** Every answer names the computer it came from, and the agent can ask for the computer's details and whether the session has administrator rights. A good first request is to ask where it is.
- **Ask for a purpose.** The agent can state in a few words why it runs something. Your customer reads that purpose next to the real command.
- **Read the results.** A failed command is reported as failed, a cancelled one as cancelled, and one that got lost as unknown. Nothing is reported as done when it is not.

## Work in their files

Your agent sends files to the customer's computer and fetches files from it. You can do the same yourself: the **Files** tab of a session is a file explorer for their computer, in your own window.

<Shot
  src={files}
  kind="wide"
  alt="The Files tab during a session: the Documents folder of the customer's computer with its folders and files, buttons to upload, download, rename and delete, and under the list the transfers of the session."
  caption="The customer's Documents folder, and under it the files that were sent and fetched."
/>

- **Send and fetch.** **Upload…**, or a drop on the window, sends files and folders to the open folder. **Download** copies what you selected to your own computer. A copy counts as done only when it is identical to the original.
- **Rename and delete** what you selected. Deleting asks first, because nothing goes to a recycle bin.
- **In sight.** Your customer sees each file that you or your agent send, fetch, rename or delete, and a file that leaves their computer is marked as such. Your audit trail records it all.

## When you are done

Press **Stop session**, or let your customer press **Stop now**. CretLink cleans up on their computer and both of you get a report. On your side the session stays under **Sessions**, with its audit trail.
